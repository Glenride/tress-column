How it works

Configure. Lock. Prove.

A certificate is not found — it is made, layer by layer, under pressure. A Tress Column launch works the same way: one careful pass at the start, then the layers settle and nothing moves again.

Step 01

Configure

The creator sets everything in one launch() transaction: total supply, the distribution split, vesting terms, lock mode, and launch guards.

The split must add up exactly — vesting + airdrop + liquidity = 100% — checked on-chain before the launch exists.

Step 02

Lock

The factory deploys the token, the pool, and the locker with those terms written into immutable fields. There is no function that writes launch state after construction.

No owner. No pause. No upgrade path. If it isn't in the contract at launch, it does not exist.

Step 03

Prove

Every launch mints exactly one certificate holding the full record in plain language — supply, splits, lock, vesting, fee mode, gate.

Written once, by the factory, in the launch transaction. Nobody has to take anyone's word for any of it.

Who does what

Six parts, one job each.

No backstage. Every actor in a Tress Column launch is named, and each one's power ends exactly where its job does.

The personCreator

Configures the launch once, in the open — then cannot change anything. Their discipline is the product.

The personTrader

Reads the certificate, then trades the pool. The terms they read are the terms the contracts enforce.

The contractFactory

Deploys everything in the launch transaction and freezes the terms into immutable fields.

The contractHook

Guards the launch window and applies the launch’s fee rule — nothing below its gate, its configured rate in quote asset above it.

The recordCertificate

The certificate itself: the full launch record, written once, kept on-chain, readable by anyone forever.

The contractLocker

Holds the liquidity. Abyssal locks have no withdrawal function at all; timed locks enforce a 180-day floor.
